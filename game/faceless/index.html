FACELESS

A surveillance-horror project that evolved from visible threats into missing frames, contradictory camera feeds, spatial audio, lighting failures, and an absence that is never fully seen.

The first generation of Faceless reached Google Play on June 26, 2026. The second generation is now in development.

Faceless began as a much rougher experimental build. Community feedback shaped its controls, environment, camera system, monster behavior, visual presentation, and eventually the identity of the horror itself.

Evolution

From a visible monster
to something between frames.

June 17, 2026
v1.41 · early beta

The rough beginning.

An older beta build was shared while a newer version was still being prepared. Early feedback called out bugs, very dark presentation, unclear interfaces, and control problems.

The team explicitly described v1.41 as an old build and said later versions had already addressed some of these issues.

June 19, 2026
v1.42 / v1.43

Community feedback becomes the roadmap.

  • Fixed monsters passing through walls.
  • Added a night background and improved interior visibility.
  • Added a central room and two inaccessible observation rooms.
  • Added an exterior camera.
  • Added a power-outage event involving the ventilation duct.
  • Added unique monster animations and sound effects.
June 19, 2026
v1.44

Story starts moving.

v1.44 added story presentation with animation and continued building on the suggestions introduced in the 1.42 and 1.43 cycle.

June 19, 2026
v1.49 · ESCAPE SIGNAL

The identity changes.

Faceless stopped depending primarily on monsters physically appearing during gameplay. The horror moved into surveillance anomalies, missing frames, contradictory camera images, directional sound, sudden lighting failures, recordings, documents, and events that cannot be directly observed.

Faceless is no longer simply a visible monster. It is the absence left by the experiment — something that exists between the missing frames.
Story Project MIRROR

Expanded references to experimental subject F-01, the escape of Faceless, missing recordings, and events inside the facility.

Audio Procedural horror

Fluorescent flicker, stressed metal, corridor whispers, numeric radio transmissions, and snapped-cable sounds.

Mobile New touch controls

SVG controls for cameras, doors, flashlight, movement, and interface closing.

Engine Godot 4.7 Stable

Forward Plus rendering, improved shadows, cinematic tonemapping, volumetric fog, moonlight, monitor glow, and localized lighting.

v1.49 was announced as the last complete open-source edition. Later versions were planned to continue as closed source.

June 19, 2026
v1.51

A Zorix voice enters the game.

The beta added a Zorix voice implementation without relying on the browser's bot voice.

Design shift

The fear moved away
from the obvious.

Early Faceless

Visible threats

Physical monsters, survival mechanics, doors, cameras, power events, and direct encounters.

Later Generation 1

Absence and contradiction

Missing frames, conflicting surveillance, directional sound, light failures, experimental records, and things the player cannot observe directly.

Do not look for Faceless.

It exists only between the missing frames.
