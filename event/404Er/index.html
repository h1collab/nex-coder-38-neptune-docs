First appearance

The event file was found before the event happened.

The earliest known copy appeared in the BASE archive with no author, no upload record, and no valid creation history. Its filename was not stable. Different systems rendered it as EVENT404?????Er, 404_ERR_EVENT, EVENT_404_NULL, or a blank name.

At the time, most of the document was empty.

Only six lines were present:

01 FIVE WILL REVIEW THIS FILE.

02 FOUR WILL ENTER.

03 FOUR WILL RETURN.

04 THREE WILL HAVE RECORDS.

05 TWO WILL REMEMBER THE FOURTH.

06 ONE WILL ASK WHY THE CAMERA WAS ALREADY RECORDING.

BASE initially classified the file as damaged metadata.

That classification lasted eleven minutes.

Prediction log

The empty sections began filling themselves.

Five BASE personnel opened the file together. Immediately afterward, a new paragraph appeared describing the room they were sitting in.

It mentioned the cracked corner of the route board, the empty cup beside Terminal 3, and a flashlight one investigator had left under the table.

None of those details existed in the archive before that moment.

Then the file added:

In forty-three seconds, Mara will say this is someone playing a joke.
Jon will stand up before she finishes.
The ceiling light nearest the door will fail.

Forty-three seconds later, all three events occurred in that order.

BASE disconnected the terminal from every available network.

The document continued updating.

Important: no known system process was writing to the file. The same additions appeared on a paper printout that had already left the room.
Escalation

It stopped predicting the room and started predicting decisions.

The document accurately recorded which personnel would argue for quarantine, who would request deletion, and which investigator would volunteer to inspect Level 29.

BASE deliberately changed plans in an attempt to invalidate the text.

The file changed first.

FILE: The expedition will be canceled at 03:11.

CONTROL NOTE: Decision made to launch at 03:10 specifically to contradict prediction.

FILE: The expedition will be launched at 03:10 because they believe this contradicts the previous sentence.

CONTROL NOTE: Operation suspended.

FILE: They will call this “suspension” rather than “cancellation.” Four will still enter.

The final sentence was considered impossible.

Four personnel entered Level 29 twenty-six minutes later.

Expedition 404-E

Four entered Level 29.

The team used the verified Level 28 route into Level 29. No attempt was made to enter the unknown /home opening. Their only task was to determine whether the archive anomaly corresponded to any physical structure.

For the first fourteen minutes, communications were normal.

Then each team member independently reported seeing a freestanding office door in different parts of the Level.

All four doors carried the same paper sign:

ARCHIVE ROOM
OCCUPIED

Control ordered the team not to open them.

One door opened by itself.

Behind it was a copy of the BASE archive room from which the mission was being monitored.

The copy was empty.

Every chair was in the correct position.

Every terminal displayed the live communications transcript.

One terminal showed text approximately nine minutes ahead of the conversation.

Nine minutes ahead

They read their own future transcript.

The team photographed the future transcript instead of reading it aloud.

The surviving image contains six legible lines:

+09:02 CONTROL: Confirm team count.

+09:05 FIELD: Four.

+09:07 CONTROL: Say your names.

+09:12 FIELD: [THREE NAMES RECORDED]

+09:14 CONTROL: Who is the fourth?

+09:17 FIELD: What fourth?

BASE immediately ordered extraction.

Nine minutes later, the exchange occurred exactly as photographed.

Return

Four people came back.

All four field suits crossed the Level 28 threshold.

All four people were physically present.

All four were counted by two independent observers.

But only three names existed on the deployment roster.

The fourth person's identification badge displayed a valid BASE format and a valid photograph, but the name field was blank.

Three returning personnel insisted the blank-badge individual had always been part of the team.

When asked for the person's name, each gave a different answer.

The blank-badge person gave a fourth answer.

No database, handwritten roster, equipment checkout sheet, training record, or previous camera archive contains an unambiguous record of the fourth person before the expedition.
Erasure

Then the archive began deleting people who were still standing in front of it.

During quarantine, ordinary records associated with the returning team began changing.

  • A group photograph lost one person without changing the spacing between the others.
  • An equipment log changed from four respirators issued to three, while four used respirators remained on the table.
  • A handwritten meal request contained four different handwriting styles but only three listed meals.
  • A door-access log recorded the same badge entering two separate rooms seventeen seconds apart.
  • One investigator's personnel file changed its status from ACTIVE to NOT FOUND while that investigator was reading it.

The affected investigator reportedly laughed once, then asked whether everyone else still remembered their surname.

Two people did.

One did not.

The investigator did not answer.

Camera 04

The worst recording was made before anyone installed the camera.

During the investigation, BASE technicians added an extra camera to the quarantine corridor. It was labeled Camera 04.

When they connected it, the storage system already contained eleven hours of footage from Camera 04.

The oldest footage was timestamped three days before the camera was purchased.

Most of it showed an empty corridor.

At 03:17 on the oldest recording, four people walked past the camera.

They were wearing the same clothing the returned expedition members would wear three days later during quarantine.

The fourth person stopped beneath the camera and looked directly into it.

The image corrupted where the face should have been.

Then the person raised one hand and held up a piece of paper.

After enhancement, the paper contained a single readable sentence:

YOU ARE WATCHING THIS
BECAUSE YOU ALREADY DID.

That piece of paper has never been found.

03:17 event

All archive clocks changed at once.

At 03:17, every active BASE terminal displayed the event file.

Systems without archive access displayed it.

Disconnected monitors displayed it.

One powered-off laptop displayed a single line for approximately four seconds despite having no battery installed.

The line read:

THREE WILL HAVE RECORDS.

Immediately afterward, the fourth returnee's temporary quarantine record disappeared.

The person did not disappear.

They were still in the room.

Camera 01 showed them.

Camera 02 showed them.

Camera 03 showed only an empty chair.

Camera 04 showed the chair occupied nine minutes before they sat down.

Final interview

The fourth returnee asked one question.

CONTROL: Tell us your name again.

RETURN-4: You won't keep it.

CONTROL: We need you to try.

RETURN-4: You asked me this already.

CONTROL: This is the first interview.

RETURN-4: Not on Camera 04.

CONTROL: How do you know what Camera 04 recorded?

RETURN-4: Because that's where you got the question.

CONTROL: What question?

RETURN-4: Why was it already recording?

The interview ends there.

The next seventeen minutes of the audio file contain room tone and four separate breathing patterns.

The written incident log says only three people remained in the room.

Cross-record contamination

Other records changed after EVENT404?????Er appeared.

BASE discovered small additions in unrelated records:

  • Event C1? briefly contained a sixth line reading ELI REMEMBERS THE FOURTH.
  • Event C112 gained a single temporary sentence: DOOR 12 IS NOT WHERE THEY WENT.
  • Level 29 displayed RECORD 404 ALREADY OPEN on an otherwise blank wall.
  • A Level 1.12 note reportedly contained the words LEAVE THE LIGHT ON FOR FOUR before returning to normal.

No current public copies retain these changes.

BASE preserved photographs of three of them.

The photographs now show blank paper.

Containment

You cannot lock a file in a room if the room is inside the file.

BASE discontinued all direct investigation of EVENT404?????Er.

Current protocol:

  1. No expedition is authorized solely because this event predicts one.
  2. No personnel count is trusted without physical confirmation from two observers.
  3. Future-dated text is photographed but not acted upon unless independently verified.
  4. Personnel whose records disappear remain people first and anomalies second.
  5. No one is isolated alone after exposure.
  6. Camera 04 is not used, replaced, destroyed, or disconnected. It is physically covered and its storage is not opened.
  7. Level 29 archive-room replicas are not entered.
Current status: containment is informational, not physical. BASE does not know whether the event is a Level 29 phenomenon, an archive phenomenon, or something that uses records to decide what becomes true.
Why the name?

EVENT404?????Er is not its original designation.

No original designation can be recovered.

Every attempt to rename the event produces a different corrupted form after synchronization. The current string—EVENT404?????Er—is simply the longest version that remains stable across paper, offline storage, and the public archive.

The suffix Er has no confirmed meaning.

One deleted draft claimed it stood for “Error.”

Another claimed it stood for “Erase.”

A third said:

IT IS THE END OF
YOUR NAME.
