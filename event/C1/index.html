The Sixth Badge Incident

BASE sent five people into Level 7. The Level prepared for six.

Mission

A normal Class 5 survey, by BASE standards.

PATHFINDER-7A entered Level 7 to verify two things: whether the collapsed prize wall still returned to Level 4, and whether a darker warehouse route beyond the emergency curtains could be mapped without prolonged exposure to the promotional hall.

BASE followed its own doctrine. No permanent base was planned. The team carried only portable light, water, first aid, chalk, rope, two cameras, and a duplicate paper roster held by RELAY personnel outside the Level.

The entry roster listed five people.

That roster still lists five people today.
First anomaly

The sixth badge.

Seven minutes after entry, body-camera footage showed the team passing a toy checkout kiosk. Six plastic visitor badges were already arranged on the counter.

Five carried the correct first names of the BASE team.

The sixth read:

WELCOME BACK, ELI

No person named Eli appeared on the deployment roster, support log, equipment issue sheet, or pre-mission briefing.

The team did not react as if the name were unfamiliar.

Instead, one member picked up the badge and said, “He forgot his again.”

When RELAY asked who “he” was, all five members paused.

T+07:14 Six visitor badges located.

T+07:21 FIELD-2: “He forgot his again.”

T+07:26 RELAY: “Who is he?”

T+07:32 [6.8 seconds silence]

T+07:39 FIELD-1: “Eli. Obviously.”

T+07:44 RELAY confirms no Eli assigned.

Escalation

Level 7 began preparing a party.

At approximately T+18 minutes, the toy aisles opened into a decorated promotion area that had not appeared in earlier surveys.

There were six chairs.

Six paper cups.

Six wrapped gifts.

Six place cards.

Five place cards matched the team. The sixth again read ELI.

A banner above the display read:

WELCOME BACK, BASE-7A.
ALL SIX OF YOU MADE IT.

The team became increasingly resistant to RELAY instructions. They insisted the sixth member had entered with them and accused outside staff of deleting him from the roster.

Their memories of Eli were inconsistent:

  • FIELD-1 said Eli was the medic.
  • FIELD-2 said Eli carried the second camera.
  • FIELD-3 remembered eating with him before deployment.
  • FIELD-4 said Eli had been with BASE for months.
  • FIELD-5 said nothing, but repeatedly turned to speak to an empty space beside them.
No sixth voice is audible on the original audio channels.
Visual record

The cameras disagree.

Camera A shows five team members throughout the mission.

Camera B contains seventeen frames in which a sixth human silhouette appears between team members. The figure is never fully visible. It is always partially blocked by balloons, cardboard displays, shelving, or another person's shoulder.

Frame analysis cannot establish whether this is a person, a Mascot Shell, a reflection, or an image artifact.

The most disturbing frame was recorded before the team reached the party display.

In it, six shadows fall across the floor.

Extraction

BASE ordered immediate withdrawal.

At T+26 minutes RELAY terminated the survey. The team was instructed to use the verified prize-wall route to Level 4.

Five voices acknowledged.

Then a sixth voice said:

UNKNOWN: Wait. Eli isn't here yet.

RELAY: Identify speaker.

FIELD-3: Stop messing around. We're waiting for him.

RELAY: There is no Eli on your team.

FIELD-1: Then who have we been talking to?

UNKNOWN: Don't leave without me.

The team ran.

Four BASE members reached Level 4 together.

A fifth arrived forty-three seconds later.

All five were physically accounted for.

Every one of them immediately asked the same question:

“Where is Eli?”
Aftermath

The memory lasted longer than the Level.

The five returning personnel were isolated separately. Without contact, all described Eli as a close teammate.

However, no two descriptions matched.

Different hair. Different age. Different role. Different voice.

One described a scar. Another insisted Eli wore glasses. A third remembered a family photograph Eli had supposedly shown them.

Forty-eight hours later, the memories began fading.

After six days, four of the five no longer believed Eli had ever existed.

The fifth refused to accept this and left a handwritten message in the BASE archive:

Maybe the scary part isn't that Level 7 invented him.
Maybe the scary part is that we were willing to abandon him once we learned he wasn't real.
Designation

Why is the event called C1?

It is not.

At least, not consistently.

The first archived copy was labeled C17. A later backup showed C1?. One paper printout recovered from the RELAY desk reads C71. No edit history explains the change.

The current archive preserves C1? because it is the only version all surviving copies partially agree on.

BASE personnel are instructed not to create or wear name badges inside Level 7.
Current BASE protocol

Level 7 remains a no-base zone.

  1. Every team enters with a paper roster duplicated outside the Level.
  2. Team size is confirmed by number, not by name, every five minutes.
  3. Unissued badges, place cards, gifts, or personalized signs are not collected.
  4. If the Level claims an extra team member exists, the expedition withdraws immediately.
  5. No missing-person search is initiated solely from Level 7-generated names.
