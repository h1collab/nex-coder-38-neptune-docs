Door 12 Contamination Incident

BASE attempted to enter Door 12 in Level 89.2 to rescue people believed to be trapped beyond it. The rescue team became mentally contaminated and entered voluntarily. The elevator was permanently sealed afterward.

Summary

The rescue attempt became part of the trap.

Door 12 had already been considered the most dangerous feature of Level 89.2. It produced voices resembling missing survivors and repeatedly encouraged people in the lobby to open the doors.

After multiple reports suggested that actual people might still be alive beyond the elevator, BASE authorized a rescue attempt. The team entered under controlled conditions with external observers, tether lines, recorded communications, and a defined withdrawal order.

The operation failed because the primary hazard was not the physical elevator.

C112 conclusion: Door 12 can create or amplify a compulsive belief that someone inside can still be saved—and that entering is the only moral choice.
Rescue attempt

“We can hear them.”

The operation began with Door 12 physically opened only far enough to confirm the presence of an elevator car. The car appeared level with the lobby floor. Its interior light was dim but stable.

Almost immediately, multiple voices were heard from deeper inside the car. Some sounded distressed. Some called for BASE directly. At least one voice reproduced phrases previously written in missing-person reports.

The field team was ordered not to advance beyond the threshold until the shaft and rear wall had been verified.

They advanced anyway.

Contamination

It did not make them afraid.

The most disturbing feature of C112 was the absence of panic. Affected BASE personnel became calmer as the operation deteriorated.

They repeatedly described the people beyond Door 12 as “close,” “waiting,” or “almost reachable.” When ordered to return, they interpreted withdrawal as abandonment rather than safety procedure.

The contamination then spread to personnel who had not crossed the threshold.

  • Observers began insisting that the field team could still be pulled out if they moved closer.
  • Personnel reported hearing individual names from inside the elevator despite audio recorders capturing only low mechanical noise.
  • One observer removed part of the restraint line because it was “hurting the people inside.”
  • Another stated that BASE's purpose was to rescue people and therefore refusing to enter would be a betrayal of BASE's mission.

The remaining contaminated personnel eventually crossed into Door 12 voluntarily.

The danger did not tell them to die.
It told them they were good people.
It told them good people would go in.

No member of the rescue group returned.

Last transmission

Final usable audio.

CONTROL: Return to the lobby. This operation is terminated.

FIELD: Negative. We found them.

CONTROL: You have not confirmed any survivor visually.

FIELD: They're right here.

CONTROL: Describe them.

FIELD: …

CONTROL: Describe who you can see.

FIELD: Why are you making us leave them?

UNKNOWN: Hold the elevator.

After the final line, all field channels transmitted the elevator arrival chime simultaneously.

Containment

Door 12 was permanently removed from service.

Following C112, the Liftkeepers and BASE abandoned every previous plan to study Door 12 from inside.

The containment measures now include:

  • Mechanical disconnection of the exterior call button.
  • Removal of accessible power and control wiring associated with Door 12.
  • Multiple welded steel plates across the door seam.
  • A secondary barrier positioned several metres in front of the elevator.
  • Floor markings defining a no-entry line.
  • Acoustic dampening intended to reduce intelligible voices from behind the doors.
  • Permanent signage referencing Event C112 rather than describing the voices in detail.
Door 12 is not an exit, route, rescue corridor, research target, or usable elevator.
BASE watch

No one watches Door 12 alone.

BASE now maintains a continuous containment watch in cooperation with the Liftkeepers. This is not considered a permanent BASE “base” inside a dangerous Level; Level 89.2 itself remains community-stabilized, while the Door 12 position is treated as a controlled local hazard.

Watch protocol requires:

  1. Minimum two-person watch. No individual remains assigned to Door 12 alone.
  2. Short rotations. Personnel are replaced before extended exposure to voices, chimes, or repetitive thoughts.
  3. No verbal response. Personnel do not answer anyone claiming to be behind Door 12.
  4. No rescue exceptions. A voice identifying itself as a missing BASE member does not authorize opening the door.
  5. Immediate relief. Any guard who says “they're still alive,” “we can get them,” or equivalent language without new evidence is removed from the watch area.
  6. Physical seal first. If the door moves, personnel retreat and alert the lobby rather than approaching it.
Current status

C112 remains active.

No confirmed survivor has emerged from Door 12 since the incident. The voices have not stopped completely.

They are now heard less often because the area is sealed and continuously supervised. On rare occasions, guards report hearing a familiar BASE voice from behind the steel plates asking a simple question:

“Why did you stop looking for us?”

Protocol requires no response.

Current classification: Door 12 — permanently sealed. Event C112 — active containment. BASE watch — continuous.
