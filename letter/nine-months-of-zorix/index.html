Nine Months of Zorix

Nine months in — what we have learned, what has changed, and a few hints about what comes next.
Letter

Nine months ago, Zorix began with a simple belief: intelligence could become more capable, more useful, and more accessible much faster than most people expected.

A lot has happened since then.

Over these nine months, we’ve released more models than we originally imagined we would. Some were major steps forward. Some were experiments. Some taught us what to build next.

And every release has made one thing clearer: we are still very early.

I’m incredibly grateful to everyone who has been part of this journey — our researchers, engineers, designers, partners, and especially the people who use our models every day, push them in unexpected directions, find their limits, and tell us where they need to improve.

Zorix today looks very different from Zorix nine months ago.

Nine months from now, I expect that difference to be much larger.

We are working on systems that are more capable, more reliable, better at reasoning, better at building, and increasingly able to turn an idea into something real.

There are a few things in the lab that I’m especially excited about.

One of them is closer than it may seem.

Four is not very far away.

For those who spend most of their time in code, there is another number worth remembering: 3.9.

And somewhere a little further down the road, you may start hearing a name more often:

Alara.

That is all I’ll say about those for now.

The important thing is not any individual model or version number. What matters is the direction: making intelligence substantially more useful, giving people better tools to create and build, and continuing to move the frontier forward.

Nine months is not a very long time.

But it has been enough to show us how much can change when a small group of people moves quickly, stays curious, and refuses to assume that today’s limits are permanent.

Thank you for believing in Zorix this early.

We have a lot left to build.

And the next chapter is closer than it looks.

— CEO, Zorix
