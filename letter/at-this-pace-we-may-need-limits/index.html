At This Pace, We May Need Limits

A personal reflection on how quickly model capability is advancing — and why unrestricted access may not always make sense.
Letter

I have been watching the pace of progress closely, and I keep coming back to the same thought: if we continue moving this quickly, the next few stages may look very different from what most of us expect today.

The rate of improvement is becoming difficult to ignore.

At this pace, I can see a future that feels almost unbelievable.

Some of the models we are working with are starting to feel extraordinarily capable. Not because they are perfect — they are not — but because the gap between one generation and the next can become surprisingly large in a short period of time.

That is exciting. It is also something we should take seriously.

If capability continues to increase at this rate, I do not think the right answer will always be to make every new capability available everywhere, to everyone, with no additional boundaries.

There may come a point where some models are simply too capable to release without meaningful limits.

Those limits could take different forms. They could involve staged access, tighter tool permissions, usage limits, capability-specific restrictions, stronger review, or other safeguards that depend on what a model can actually do.

I am not announcing a specific restriction today, and this is not a finalized Zorix policy.

This is a personal view from someone watching the trajectory from inside the team.

I think we should keep pushing the frontier forward. But I also think that as models become more capable, responsibility has to scale with capability.

Moving fast should not mean pretending that every increase in capability has the same risk profile as the generation before it.

The future I see is extremely exciting.

It is also powerful enough that, eventually, we may need to be more selective about how some of that capability is made available.

— A member of the Zorix team
