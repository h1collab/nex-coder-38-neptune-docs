B/ASE
Boundary Aid & Survey Expedition

Explore.
Map.
Bring people back.

BASE is a survivor-led exploration and relief organization operating across NULVORIA. Its work is simple to describe and difficult to do: find routes, record hazards, carry supplies, locate missing people, and make sure useful knowledge reaches the next person.

BASE doctrine: dangerous Levels are explored, not occupied.
Mission

BASE exists for the person who arrives next.

BASE teams document entrances and exits, verify survival conditions, mark reliable routes, carry emergency supplies, help injured or lost survivors, and share observations with the public NULVORIA archive.

The organization does not claim ownership of Levels and does not attempt to control independent survivor communities. Its purpose is practical assistance.

01

Explore

Enter undocumented or poorly understood areas with a defined return plan.

02

Survey

Record routes, hazards, entities, resources, timing patterns, and structural changes.

03

Assist

Provide water, light, first aid, route guidance, and evacuation help.

04

Return

No discovery is worth losing an entire team. Information only matters if someone brings it back.

Base doctrine

No permanent base in a dangerous Level.

BASE distinguishes between permanent stations, temporary field points, and no-hold zones.

CLASS 0

Permanent base allowed

Long-duration shelter, supply storage, medical support, route documentation, and overnight staffing may be established if the local area remains stable.

CLASS 1

Permanent base allowed

Stations may operate with continuous route checks and a known evacuation path.

CLASS 2

Restricted base

Small staffed stations are permitted only in verified low-risk pockets with at least two exits.

CLASS 3

Temporary staging only

No permanent sleeping quarters. Equipment caches and short rescue stops may exist only while a team is active.

CLASS 4

No base

Exploration teams enter with what they carry and leave with what they learn. No permanent personnel or stockpile is left behind.

CLASS 5

No base

Rescue and survey operations are time-limited. Every mission requires a withdrawal trigger before entry.

UNDEFINED

No base

Unknown, corrupted, recursive, or structurally unstable Levels are treated as no-hold zones until proven otherwise.

Examples: BASE does not establish permanent bases in Level 5, Level 7, Level 16, Level 18, Level 26, Level 29, Level 3.141, or Level 5.61.

Field teams

Small teams, defined roles.

PATHFINDER

Route team

Tests entrances, exits, loops, timing windows, and structural drift. Never travels without a return marker.

LANTERN

Relief team

Carries water, light, first aid, food, blankets, and simple navigation tools to stranded survivors.

RELAY

Record team

Keeps duplicate notes outside the target Level so a lost expedition does not erase everything it discovered.

RETURN

Extraction team

Waits at the known exit or fallback point and begins retrieval when a field team's return window is missed.

Danger protocol

What BASE leaves in places where it cannot stay.

In dangerous Levels, permanent infrastructure can become a trap. BASE therefore uses low-profile markers that can be abandoned without endangering future visitors.

  • Chalk arrows with a timestamp and team code.
  • Reflective tape marking verified return routes.
  • Small sealed emergency caches only when retrieval is realistic.
  • Written hazard cards placed before a known danger, never beyond it.
  • No beds, permanent lights, large food stores, generators, or staffed posts.
A BASE symbol does not mean the Level is safe. It means somebody surveyed that exact point and returned.

Principle

A base is not a flag.

BASE does not build stations to claim territory. A station exists only where staying helps people more than leaving would.

When a Level becomes too unstable, the organization evacuates. When a route becomes unsafe, the old marking is crossed out. When another community already keeps people alive—as in Level 89.2—BASE supports them instead of replacing them.
