Summary

It does not feel like one Level.

Level 3.141 is one of the largest documented special environments in NULVORIA. Survivors do not enter a single room or district. Instead they pass through a sequence of radically different spaces that appear to be arranged along an invisible curve.

The farther one travels, the more frequently corridors bend, roads arc, staircases return to themselves, and signs display longer strings of digits. Measurements suggest no consistent radius. Attempts to walk in a straight line eventually produce a leftward curve even when walls and landmarks indicate otherwise.

Primary archive rule: if you can see where the path ends, assume that is not where it ends.
General rules

Five rules survived every expedition.

  1. Do not follow music that becomes clearer while you walk away from it.
  2. Do not answer voices that know your name but cannot describe your face.
  3. If a road sign shows more than six digits of π, do not continue on that road.
  4. Never sleep in a room with balloons, streamers, or a lit EXIT sign.
  5. If the clock changes from 03:14 to 03:17 without passing through the minutes in between, move immediately.
Zone A
A / 3.1410–3.1419

The Black Corridor.

The first zone resembles maintenance corridors beneath Level 3 after all lighting has failed. The darkness is unusually complete. Flashlights illuminate only a short cone, and reflective surfaces appear to absorb more light than they return.

Walls are cold, smooth, and mostly featureless except for occasional painted arrows. These arrows should not be trusted: several lead directly back to the location where they were first seen.

Observed phenomena

  • Footsteps may continue for several seconds after the visitor stops walking.
  • Breathing can be heard from behind walls too thin to contain another corridor.
  • Flashlights sometimes reveal a second shadow moving out of synchronization with the first.
  • Distance becomes unreliable; a door ten metres away can require twenty minutes to reach.

Entity: The Quiet Ones

Human-sized silhouettes occasionally appear at the edge of flashlight range. They do not approach while directly illuminated. When the light moves away, their distance usually decreases.

Survivors report that counting aloud from 1 to 10 while keeping the light pointed forward reduces encounters. No explanation is known.
Zone B
B / 3.142–3.15

The Night Road.

Zone B opens abruptly onto a suburban road beneath a moonless sky. Houses line both sides, but almost all windows are dark. Streetlights operate in groups of three, leaving long unlit gaps between them.

The road never appears to curve visually, yet compass readings rotate slowly. Walking forward for long enough eventually reveals the same bus stop from the opposite direction.

Hazards

  • Cars sometimes pass without engine noise. Looking inside is strongly discouraged.
  • Some homes display a porch light only after the visitor looks away.
  • Street names become strings such as 314159, 265358, or 979323.
  • A distant figure may walk on the opposite pavement while perfectly matching the visitor's pace.

Entity: The Resident Across the Road

A tall person-shaped figure sometimes appears behind a window in one of the houses. It remains still until the visitor reaches the next streetlight. At that point, it may appear in a closer house.

Never enter a house whose front door is already open. Every recorded team that did so lost contact within minutes.
Zone C
C / 3.16–3.18

The Celebration Hall.

Zone C resembles an enormous indoor family-entertainment centre combined with a closed supermarket promotion floor. Colored lights flash without a visible controller. Tables are set with paper plates. Balloons remain inflated indefinitely.

Unlike most of Level 3.141, this zone is warm, brightly lit, and appears welcoming. This is precisely why the archive considers it dangerous.

The invitation effect

Visitors often hear familiar voices calling from behind decorated doors. These voices may imitate family, friends, or deceased people. They usually ask the visitor to “come in,” “sit down,” or “stay for five minutes.”

  • Food on the tables smells normal but should not be eaten.
  • Birthday banners sometimes display the visitor's current age, then one number higher.
  • Music becomes louder when a person attempts to leave.
  • Confetti may fall upward toward the ceiling.

Entity: The Hosts

The Hosts resemble distant people wearing bright party clothing. Their faces cannot be clearly remembered after looking away. They rarely chase immediately. Instead, they attempt to keep visitors inside through conversation, music, food, and false familiarity.

Do not stay for the final song. No complete recording of it exists.
Zone D
D / 3.19–3.140

The Service Tower.

After leaving the bright zone, visitors typically enter a huge industrial structure containing electrical rooms, lift shafts, ventilation spaces, water pipes, machine halls, steel stairs, and service bridges extending vertically beyond sight.

This is the most physically dangerous region. Heat, moving machinery, open shafts, live cables, and long climbs make exhaustion common.

Environmental anomalies

  • Staircases can gain or lose floors between landings.
  • Elevator indicators show decimal values rather than floor numbers.
  • Maintenance maps depict a circular tower, but windows show straight exterior walls.
  • Some machines continue operating after every visible power cable is cut.

Entity: Maintenance

“Maintenance” refers to worker-shaped figures wearing reflective vests and opaque face shields. They usually ignore visitors unless machinery is touched or a restricted door is forced open.

If one points toward an exit, do not assume it is helping. Maintenance appears more interested in restoring the level's preferred configuration than in protecting people.

The only consistently useful resources in this zone are sealed water containers, work gloves, batteries, and emergency blankets.
Zone E
E / π

The Pi Core.

The final documented zone is a circular space so large that the opposite side is hidden by atmospheric haze. Concentric walkways descend toward a dark central opening. Along the walls, illuminated numbers continue in both directions.

The digits match π for millions of positions before eventually diverging. Where they diverge, survivors have reported seeing dates, names, and fragments of sentences embedded among the numbers.

The centre

No expedition has reached the central opening. Walking toward it reduces the apparent distance slowly at first, then not at all. Looking backward reveals the entrance much farther away than the distance already travelled.

  • Digital devices lose time but not battery.
  • Spoken words may repeat several minutes later from somewhere lower in the spiral.
  • The number 03:17 appears unusually often inside digit sequences where it should not statistically dominate.
  • Some survivors have found their own birth dates followed immediately by EXIT.
Do not descend after finding your own name in the digits.

Maintenance

Worker-like figures that enforce unknown structural rules.

Entrances

Recorded access points.

  • In Level 3, follow a freezer corridor whose temperature display changes to 3.141°C. A black service door may appear at the end.
  • In Level 6, walking a fuel-station road while every pump display reads 3.14 can lead directly to Zone B.
  • In Level 9, entering a pharmacy consultation room labeled PI has produced Zone A twice.
  • In Level 12, a circular loading ramp that completes more than one full turn without changing elevation may descend into Zone D.
Exits

Leaving the spiral.

  • A lit freezer door in Zone A can return to Level 3.
  • A bus stop in Zone B displaying route 314 has returned survivors to Level 6.
  • Leaving Zone C before the music restarts may open into Level 7 or Level 10.
  • Emergency stairs marked 3F in Zone D may lead to Level 12.
  • In the Pi Core, a door labeled only 0 has returned three survivors to Level 0.
False exits are common. Any EXIT sign that flashes in rhythm with music should be treated as an entrance deeper into the level.
Expedition 3.141-A

“We thought the bright room meant we were safe.”

The first archive team to document all five zones entered from Level 3 with four members and returned with two.

The team spent approximately nine hours in darkness before reaching the night road. Their written notes remain coherent until Zone C.

At 03:17 according to every surviving watch, the team recorded music coming from a decorated hall. One member wrote that the room smelled like a birthday party from childhood. Another insisted there was someone inside who knew him.

The audio record contains a voice saying, “You're late. We kept your seat.”

Thirty seconds later, one team member entered the decorated room. The others followed only far enough to see tables prepared for exactly four people.

One place card already had the missing member's name printed on it.

The other three ran.

Only two reached the Service Tower.

When they later entered the Pi Core, both found the missing member's name inside a sequence of digits.

The digits immediately following it were:

3 1 4 1 5 9 — STILL HERE

Neither survivor agreed to return.

Archive interpretation

Why 3.141?

The current theory is not that NULVORIA is “using mathematics” in any conventional sense. Instead, Level 3.141 appears to use the idea of a curve that never closes, a number that never ends, and a path that can continue indefinitely without repeating exactly.

The five zones may therefore be different expressions of the same rule: darkness without visible end, roads without destination, celebrations that never conclude, machinery that never finishes its cycle, and a spiral that cannot reach its centre.

The archive currently classifies Level 3.141 as one of the most structurally complex known NULVORIA environments.
