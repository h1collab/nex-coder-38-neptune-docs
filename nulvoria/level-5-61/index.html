Overview

Language breaks first.

Level 5.61 begins like a damaged branch of Level 5. Vending machines, cooler doors, shelf labels, price displays, and advertisement screens remain recognizable, but text rapidly deteriorates into malformed characters.

The corruption is not limited to screens. Printed receipts, handwritten notes, spoken words, and eventually remembered names can become “misdecoded.” Survivors describe knowing that a sign once said something important while being unable to recover the original word.

Archive rule: write your name on paper before entering. Do not rely on remembering its spelling.
Corruption stages

The text gets worse the longer you stay.

Stage 1

Visual noise

Letters duplicate, punctuation shifts, and labels display fragments such as Ãƒ, Â, or �.

Stage 2

Semantic drift

Readable words remain, but their meaning no longer matches the object they label.

Stage 3

Speech loss

Voices may sound normal while listeners perceive them as broken subtitles floating near the speaker.

Stage 4

Identity corruption

Names, memories, and familiar phrases become difficult to reconstruct. Immediate exit is recommended.

Entity

The Decoder.

The Decoder is not consistently visible. It is usually detected through corrections: a damaged sign briefly becomes readable, a garbled voice suddenly becomes clear, or a broken label rearranges itself when no one is touching it.

When seen directly, survivors describe a human-sized shape made from overlapping blocks of letters, barcode fragments, and screen pixels. It does not appear immediately hostile.

The danger begins when it “fixes” a person. Survivors who remain close to the entity report forgetting one word at a time—first trivial nouns, then names, then personal details.

If a perfectly readable sentence appears in an otherwise corrupted area and asks for your name, do not answer.
Entrances

Entering 5.61.

  • In Level 5, use a vending machine whose price display reads 5.61 while every product label is corrupted.
  • Open an OUT OF ORDER machine after its screen changes to UTF-8?.
  • Rarely, reading a heavily corrupted receipt aloud in Level 4 produces a service corridor leading here.
Exits

Recover something readable.

  • A cooler door labeled correctly as WATER can return to Level 5.
  • A receipt containing a fully readable timestamp 03:17 may open a route to Level 4.
  • If every sign suddenly becomes perfect at the same time, do not use the nearest exit. This event is associated with The Decoder.
The safest known exit method is to compare multiple handwritten labels and follow the first word that remains unchanged across all copies.
Recovered note

“I forgot the word for door.”

A survivor identified only as M. recorded twenty-seven short voice notes while leaving Level 5.61.

The first recordings are clear. By the nineteenth, M. repeatedly pauses before ordinary nouns. In the twenty-fourth, they say:

I know what it is. The thing you open. The thing in the wall. I can see it. I just don't have the sound anymore.

The twenty-seventh recording contains only breathing and one correctly pronounced phrase:

03:17

M. later reached Level 5 and recovered most language ability after several hours. The word “door” was the last to return.
