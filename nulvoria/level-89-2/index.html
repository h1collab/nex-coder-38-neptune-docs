Main lobby

The elevator room, in detail.

Level 89.2 consists of a wide elevator lobby built from beige stone panels, brushed stainless steel, dark green carpet, and a ceiling grid of warm fluorescent fixtures. The room is approximately thirty metres long, but its width changes by a few centimetres between measurements.

There are twelve elevator doors on the main wall. Above each is an old red floor indicator. Eight cars are currently considered usable. Two are mechanically sealed. One is monitored but not trusted. The final door—Door 12—is now under permanent C112 containment: welded steel plates cover the seam, a secondary barrier keeps people back, and BASE personnel maintain a continuous watch.

Between the elevators are handmade objects added over time: chalk arrows, a cork noticeboard, shelves of bottled water, a battery charging table, spare flashlights, a first-aid cabinet, a mechanical bell, and dozens of handwritten destination reports.

The lobby now feels inhabited. That is deliberate.

Door 12

C112 containment

After a failed BASE rescue attempt caused mental contamination and further disappearances, Door 12 was permanently sealed. It is monitored continuously and is no longer considered a usable elevator.

Before

Why it used to be Class 5.

Early records describe Level 89.2 as one of the most lethal transit environments in the archive. Elevator doors opened without cars. Floors arrived misaligned by several metres. Indicators displayed impossible negative values. Some cars descended for hours before returning to the same lobby.

Worst of all, no shared map existed. Survivors arrived alone, pressed random buttons, and disappeared independently. A person could learn a dangerous route only by surviving it—and that knowledge usually vanished with them.

  • Door 6 opened repeatedly into an uncovered shaft.
  • Car 9 sometimes opened while still moving.
  • Door 12 produced voices asking waiting survivors to “hold the elevator.”
  • The call chime could sound even when no car existed behind the doors.
  • Floor indicators frequently displayed the visitor's age instead of a floor.
The original Class 5 rating was not caused by a single entity. It was caused by the combination of unstable machinery, isolation, missing information, and repeated human error.
The change

They stopped treating survival as an individual problem.

The first permanent change began with six survivors who refused to leave the lobby separately.

They tested one elevator at a time.

One person watched the doors. One wrote down the indicator sequence. One held a rope line. One waited at the lobby with a bell. Two remained ready to pull someone back if the floor was not level.

Nothing dramatic happened at first. They simply wrote things down.

Car 2 reached Level 8 safely three times.

Car 6 opened onto a shaft twice, so they blocked it.

Car 4 was safe only when its indicator stopped on an even number.

Door 12 imitated the voices of people who had already disappeared.

They wrote that down too.

Then other survivors arrived.

Someone brought tools. Someone else brought a first-aid kit. A mechanic stabilized the inner door of Car 5. Two people built a wooden barrier in front of Door 12. Another survivor painted the first green line on the floor showing where it was safe to stand while a car arrived.

The wall slowly filled with knowledge.

If you learn something, write it down.
If you survive a route, mark it.
If someone is scared, don't send them alone.
If the door looks wrong, believe the person beside you.

Over time, the lobby stopped being a trap people passed through and became a place where people waited for one another.

The Level itself did not become normal. The elevators still misbehave. Door 12 is still dangerous. Unknown floors still appear.

What changed was that almost nobody has to face those things alone anymore.

The Liftkeepers

A community built around a call button.

The Liftkeepers are not an authority in the traditional sense. They are volunteers, route recorders, mechanics, cooks, medics, and people who simply stayed long enough to help the next person.

A typical staffed shift maintains three positions:

Door watch
  • Checks floor alignment.
  • Verifies a car is physically present.
  • Stops visitors from approaching sealed doors.
Route desk
  • Records destinations and return times.
  • Updates chalk markings.
  • Compares reports for route drift.

A third person stays near the mechanical bell. One ring means a car has returned. Two rings mean help is needed. Three rapid rings mean everyone must move away from the elevator wall.

Event C112

The rescue attempt that ended Door 12 research.

BASE attempted to enter Door 12 to rescue people believed to be trapped beyond it. The field team became mentally contaminated, refused withdrawal orders, and entered voluntarily. The contamination then affected supporting personnel, who followed them inside.

Afterward, Door 12 was permanently sealed. BASE now maintains a continuous two-person watch in cooperation with the Liftkeepers.

Safety protocol

The rules are written above the benches.

  1. Look through the floor gap before stepping forward.
  2. Do not enter a car whose interior light is red.
  3. Never respond to a voice behind Door 12. Event C112 established that familiar voices can produce rescue compulsion.
  4. Write your destination and departure time on the board.
  5. If the indicator shows your age, birthday, or name, step away from the doors.
  6. No first-time visitor rides alone.
  7. If someone does not return, their route stays marked until proven unsafe or safe.
Resources

The small things made it livable.

  • Two benches have been converted into sleeping platforms.
  • Water is stored beneath the route desk.
  • A metal cabinet contains bandages, gloves, rope, batteries, and chalk.
  • One wall outlet provides intermittent power and is reserved for radios and lights.
  • A handwritten shelf labeled TAKE ONE / LEAVE ONE contains food and small tools.
Nothing here is infinite. The Level is safer because people keep maintaining it.
Entrances

How to reach 89.2.

  • In Level 26, an elevator button marked 89.2 occasionally appears after a safe return trip.
  • Some service lifts in Level 12 open directly into the lobby when their indicator shows 89 and then a small decimal point.
  • Survivors carrying route notes from another person appear slightly more likely to arrive here than those travelling alone.
Exits

A safer transit hub, not a guaranteed one.

  • Green Car 2 has a frequently verified route to Level 8.
  • Green Car 5 often reaches Level 17.
  • Car 7 can return to Level 26.
  • Amber Car 4 has reached Level 1, Level 10, and unknown lobbies; it is used only with route monitoring.
  • No exit through Door 12 is considered valid.
Archive conclusion

Level 89.2 is still dangerous.

The classification changed because the conditions of survival changed.

The shafts are still there. The false chimes are still there. Some elevators still arrive at places that should not exist.

But there are now marks on the floor telling people where to stand. There is a person checking whether the car is actually there. There is a rope tied to the railing. There is a board filled with names of people who returned.

And when the doors open, someone is usually waiting on this side.

Current archive classification: Class 1, community-stabilized. The Level did not become kind. People made it survivable.
